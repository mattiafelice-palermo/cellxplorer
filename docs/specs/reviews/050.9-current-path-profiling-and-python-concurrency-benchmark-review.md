## Reviewer assessment of the final results

The final 050.9 evidence is now decision-useful rather than just timing-rich.

For the real 11-Cell All/Time case, the sequential backend median is about **1.01 s**. Whole-Cell Python threads at the corrected resolved boundary reduce the composed total to about **0.92 s with 2 workers** and **0.90 s with 4 workers** on this machine. That gain is workload-specific, not universal.

Broad derivatives move in the opposite direction: real six-Cell dQ/dV rises from about **2.03 s** sequential to about **2.15 s** with 2 workers and **2.49 s** with 4 workers. Any later production composition therefore needs workload-aware gating rather than an unconditional Cell thread pool.

Read/decode concurrency is a smaller effect. The corrected isolated read stage improves by roughly **5-9% median** overall, but representative total requests get slower. It should therefore be treated as a secondary composable mechanism to revisit in 050.11, not as a standalone production win.

The strongest remaining backend target is still the derivative kernel. Six-Cell dQ/dV spends roughly **0.88 s** (controlled) to **1.25 s** (real) in the derivative stage, which is sufficient evidence to proceed with the 050.10 sequential-Rust vs Rayon kernel benchmark.
