## Findings

### R1 — High — corrected worker boundary is now safe, but owner setup is still excluded from the candidate timing and the decision overgeneralizes across workloads

**Affected files**

- `scripts/profile_time_capacity_concurrency.py`
- `docs/specs/050.9-current-path-profiling-and-python-concurrency-benchmark.md`

**Current**

The substantive thread-safety/parity part of R1 is fixed. `_whole_cell_task()` now receives owner-resolved `ReadJob`/`ResolvedRequest` data, creates no SQLAlchemy Session, performs the per-Cell read plus transforms, and `_merge_whole_cell_results()` independently assembles traces, settings, voltage facts, provenance and rendering rather than borrowing those fields from A0.

However, `profile_workload()` still calls `build_read_jobs(...)` before `run_whole_cell_candidate()` starts its timer. `build_read_jobs()` performs the required owner-side work: ORM Cell/source resolution, source preload, scalar metadata lookup, parser-version resolution, stitch-plan construction, requested-cycle resolution, voltage-context resolution and descriptor construction. That cost is neither included in B3/B4 `backend_wall_ms` nor recorded as a separate owner/request setup measurement.

A0 `backend_wall_ms`, by contrast, is the full sequential production request and includes relational/request setup. The current total comparison therefore gives B3/B4 a necessary setup phase for free. The reported real 11-Cell result (A0 1062.4 ms vs B3 938.6 ms / B4 896.2 ms) cannot yet be interpreted as an end-to-end backend gain until the once-per-request owner setup is measured and composed with the worker/merge phase.

The corrected measurements also show a material workload split that the single overall `useful` label hides. Normal broad multi-Cell rows improve, but six-Cell dQ/dV regresses: controlled 1588.5 ms A0 → 1663.8/1923.8 ms B3/B4, and real 2132.5 ms → 2225.4/2655.5 ms. A median-across-all-workloads label of “useful at both” is therefore not a safe universal conclusion for later production composition.

**Target**

Keep the corrected no-ORM worker boundary, but measure the owner/request setup once outside the worker pool and report it explicitly. Report both the isolated worker-phase scaling and a composed candidate total that includes the necessary once-per-request setup plus worker/merge work, so the candidate total can be compared fairly with A0.

Update the 050.9 decision from the corrected evidence without flattening materially different workload classes. At minimum distinguish normal Time/Capacity multi-Cell behavior from broad derivative behavior so 050.10/050.11 do not treat whole-Cell Python threading as a universally beneficial execution policy.

**Acceptance criteria**

- B3/B4 workers continue to receive no live ORM objects or SQLAlchemy sessions and perform no relational selection/source-resolution work.
- The once-per-request owner setup used to build resolved descriptors/plans is timed and reported separately.
- A composed B3/B4 candidate total includes that required owner setup exactly once, plus worker execution and deterministic merge/assembly, and is compared against the full A0 request on the same basis.
- Worker-only scaling may remain separately reported for attribution.
- Candidate parity remains independently produced and deterministic.
- The durable decision explicitly records the observed normal-versus-derivative split; do not describe B3/B4 as a universal backend speedup when broad derivative rows regress materially.
- Rerun/recalculate the controlled and real representative results, including the 11-Cell All/Time case, and update the implementation record from the final accepted measurements.
- Focused tests and canonical `python scripts\preflight.py` pass before handoff.

### R2 — Medium — durable B1/B2 evidence exists now, but the isolated read-stage comparison still uses mismatched timing boundaries

**Affected files**

- `scripts/profile_time_capacity_concurrency.py`
- `docs/specs/050.9-current-path-profiling-and-python-concurrency-benchmark.md`

**Current**

The missing durable evidence from round 1 is fixed: the implementation record now reports B1/B2 read-stage medians/ratios, the real 11-Cell workload, a controlled broad workload, and the separate total-backend effect.

The classification itself is still biased by a boundary mismatch. `_stage_summary()` defines A0 `raw_read_decode_ms` as the sum of `index_stitch_plan`, `indexed_raw_access`, `legacy_full_raw_read`, and `prepared_derived_read`. B1/B2 `read_wall_ms`, however, starts inside `run_read_candidate()` only after `build_read_jobs()` has already constructed the stitch plan and requested-cycle mapping for every Cell.

Thus B1/B2 are compared as “parallel materialization/decode after planning” against an A0 denominator that also includes serial index/stitch planning. The claimed paired median improvements (-21.8% B1 / -20.8% B2, and -26.6%/-25.5% in the real 11-Cell example) may therefore include work omitted from the candidate timer rather than concurrency gain alone.

**Target**

Make the isolated read/decode comparison boundary-identical. Either compare B1/B2 `read_wall_ms` against an A0 materialization/decode measure that excludes `index_stitch_plan`, or include the equivalent plan-construction work in the B1/B2 timed stage. Planning may be reported separately, but it must not appear only in the denominator of the concurrency ratio.

Regenerate the compact privacy-safe evidence and decision from the corrected like-for-like measurement. Continue to state total backend effect separately.

**Acceptance criteria**

- B1/B2 and A0 read-stage ratios compare the same work boundary.
- `index_stitch_plan`/owner planning time is either excluded from both sides of the isolated read/decode comparison or included in both sides.
- Recompute the paired median read-stage percentage change for B1 and B2 after the boundary correction.
- Keep representative controlled broad and real 11-Cell read-stage values plus total-backend values in the implementation record.
- If the corrected ratio no longer crosses the 5% threshold, update the decision accordingly rather than preserving the prior label.

## Reviewer assessment of the corrected results

The corrected worker experiment is much more informative than round 1. It demonstrates that ordinary Python threads can improve the **normal multi-Cell read+transform worker phase** on this machine: the real 11-Cell All/Time worker result falls from roughly 1.06 s sequential to roughly 0.94/0.90 s before accounting for owner setup. Conversely, broad derivatives become slower under the same whole-Cell threading boundary, particularly at four workers. That strongly suggests any later production strategy should be workload-aware rather than a single unconditional pool policy.

The read-prefetch result still appears secondary even before the R2 accounting correction: its total backend effect is neutral to slightly slower in the representative rows. The main architectural value of B1/B2 is therefore likely as a composable I/O-stage optimization only if 050.11 shows it adds to a stronger transform/kernel strategy.

The derivative handoff to 050.10 remains well motivated. Six-Cell dQ/dV spends about 0.89 s (controlled) to 1.30 s (real) in the derivative stage, making that a materially larger target than the read/decode stage.
